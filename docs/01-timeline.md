# Timeline, ambition, and uncertainty

The primary hands-on route is now the [24-week experienced-engineer bootcamp](00-personalized-bootcamp.md). The 40-week estimates below describe the fuller course and its original portfolio/research-engineer target; they are not extra weeks added to the bootcamp.

## What “minimum time” can honestly mean

No study-hour formula can predict when a frontier lab will recruit a particular person or offer exceptional compensation. Starting ability, research outcomes, prior experience, team needs, geography, interviewing, and external recognition all matter. Public job postings do not establish a time-to-mastery curve. The estimates below are **judgment-based planning ranges**, conditional on passing the assessments.

The controllable objective is independent research-engineer readiness. A much stronger outcome—being sought out for unusually valuable research or systems expertise—requires external evidence of impact. A course can help create that evidence but cannot schedule its discovery or reception.

The default 40 weeks is approximately 9.2 months; 48 weeks is approximately 11 months. Allow **9–12 calendar months** with normal interruptions. The fastest advanced route is approximately 6–8 months. These are estimates for readiness, not dates for an offer or exhaustive mastery of LLM research.

For the exceptional recruiting goal, keep a second planning horizon of **12–24+ months from a strong technical starting point** for sustained research, external collaborations, and recruiting cycles. This is a resource-planning horizon, not an expected offer date; the outcome may take years or never occur. Do not delay worthwhile applications until someone recruits you unsolicited.

| 10:00–10:30 | Break |
| 10:30–12:30 | Implementation: 2 h |
| 12:30–13:30 | Lunch and walk |
| 13:30–15:30 | Experiments and debugging: 2 h |
| 15:30–16:30 | Exercise and recovery |
| 16:30–18:30 | Analysis, targeted reading, review: 2 h |
| 18:30–19:00 | Dinner/break |
| 19:00–20:00 | Explain results, log, plan next experiment: 1 h |

Five days produce 45 hours. Saturday has 7 flexible hours for assessment, review, cleanup, and catch-up. Sunday is off. Keep sleep and personal time outside the window. If you meant 12 net coursework hours rather than 12 available hours, extra time is optional; do not assume 72–84 hours/week shortens the timeline proportionally.

A representative 52-hour week: 28 implementation/experiments, 8 math/reading, 6 analysis/writing, 6 assessment/review, 4 collaboration/interviews. Computer run time does not count as study time when you are away. Waiting for a GPU is not a reason to delay CPU tests or analysis.

## Shortening and extending the plan

To skip a module, pass its gate on a fresh task, defend the choices orally, and provide an independently produced artifact. Watching related lectures is insufficient. A possible **24-week advanced route** is: 2 weeks to demonstrate Modules 1–3; 4 for systems; 4 for scaling/evaluation; 3 to demonstrate SFT/RL foundations; 3 for RLHF/reasoning gaps; 8 for the capstone. It is available only when existing evidence replaces omitted learning, not when assignments are silently dropped.

Reforecast at Weeks 2, 6, 10, 14, 18, 24, 28, 32, and 40. Record remaining failed gates, recent throughput of accepted work, budget, and the next bottleneck. Estimate remaining weeks from unfinished module allocations, adjust for demonstrated skills, then add specific remediation time. Do not advance past a dependency you cannot explain.

One failed gate: spend 2–5 study days repairing it, then retry a new variant. Two failures on the same dependency: add a focused bridge block. If reserve time is exhausted, extend the finish date. A null research result can pass; broken methodology cannot.

If independent recall, debugging quality, or assignment completion deteriorates for two consecutive weeks, reduce the load and reassess. This is a course-management rule, not a medical claim.

## What remains outside an at-home course

Small runs expose algorithms and many engineering problems, but do not substitute for operating a frontier-scale cluster. Obtain collaborative production or research experience where possible. Distinguish measured single-device, multi-device, and multi-node experience on your résumé. The [career guide](05-career.md) maps this gap to practical next steps.
