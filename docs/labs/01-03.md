## Walkthrough: create a controlled image experiment

Generate 16×16 grayscale images with either a horizontal or a vertical bar. Use balanced labels. Start with unit-valued bars on a zero background, then add a recorded amount of Gaussian noise. Inspect at least 20 examples and verify that the generator and labels agree before training.

Use positions 2 through 11 for training, some additional examples at those positions for validation, and positions 12–13 for a protected spatial-transfer test. Keep an ordinary held-out sample from familiar positions as a separate test of the in-distribution setting. Make the split manifest explicit rather than describing all unseen images as one condition.

Compare a small flattened-input MLP with a convolutional model such as `Conv2d(1,8,3,padding=1) → ReLU → AdaptiveAvgPool2d(1) → Flatten → Linear(8,2)`. Use equal data access and clearly reported parameter counts and training budgets. The architectures need not have identical parameters to teach the point, but a claim of architectural superiority requires a more careful controlled study.

Walk through one convolution: the same small filter is applied at different spatial locations. This weight sharing expresses a useful assumption about images. Pooling can make the classifier less dependent on absolute position, but it can also discard information a task needs.

Evaluate accuracy separately by orientation, bar position, and noise level. Save representative failures. Do not infer that a tiny synthetic experiment establishes performance on real images or every type of distribution shift.

**If stuck:** display tensors in their actual channel/height/width order, check label balance, and first overfit ten images. Ensure that augmentations cannot change the orientation label without updating it.

**Independent check:** add a task where absolute position determines the label. Predict why translation-insensitive pooling may now be unhelpful, and test that prediction with the smallest useful comparison.
